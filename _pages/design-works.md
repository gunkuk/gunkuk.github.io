<summary><strong>Megastructure</strong> — Material System &amp; Superstructure</summary>

<div style="margin-top:1rem;">
<p><em>Yonsei University Studio X · Year 3, Fall 2024</em></p>
<p>The superstructure idea asks how a massive system can emerge from very simple per-unit rules and parametric variations responding to environment — termite mounds and fish schools as canonical references. The project develops a <strong>hypar-based</strong> (hyperbolic paraboloid) unit system that, through parametric control, simultaneously (i) minimizes bending across the megastructure, (ii) maximizes constructability, and (iii) responds to per-space requirements such as height, enclosure geometry, and daylight.</p>
<p><strong>Material logic — bending-free aggregation.</strong> When hypars are joined under G1 tangent continuity + coplanarity, the membrane (bending-free) behavior of a single hypar is preserved across the whole combination. A bare combination behaves like an arch–cable hybrid, so multiple hypars are aggregated into an inhabitable unit. The unit is controlled by branching parameters, base-polygon parameters, height/area ratios, and an environment-responsive mechanism.</p>
<p><strong>Site application — Yeouido Park.</strong> Programs required for the urban void of Yeouido Park are identified (treating the park as a <em>receptive void</em>) and laid out across the site. Each program defines a virtual envelope; the floor plan is partitioned via a diffusion-based logic, and the superstructure is generated to match. The resulting structure carries bending only at the foundation piles.</p>

<summary><strong>EHLA House &amp; Office</strong> — Concrete Tectonics with Seoul Skyline</summary>

<div style="margin-top:1rem;">
<p><em>Yonsei University Studio X · Year 3, Spring 2024</em></p>
<p>The studio brief required combining <strong>residential and office programs</strong> within a single building. Rather than dividing the two programs purely in plan, the project pursues an <strong>interlocked vertical–horizontal relationship</strong> between them. The site is set on a steep terraced hillside in Jongno-gu, Seoul — a <em>chukdae</em> (retaining-wall) neighborhood built up in stacked stone terraces, with sharp grade changes on all four sides. The terrain is harnessed so that an open, terraced street-level office and a residence beneath it form an organic, layered relationship rather than two stacked floors.</p>

<summary><strong>Study after Valerio Olgiati</strong> — <em>House for a Priest</em></summary>

<div style="margin-top:1rem;">
<p><em>Yonsei University Studio X · Year 3, Spring 2024</em></p>
<p>A reconstruction-style study of Valerio Olgiati's <em>House for a Priest</em>. With publicly available drawings and information limited, the work proceeds as a hands-on exercise in <strong>re-deriving how the building might actually have been put together</strong> — testing material decisions, structural logic, and detailing as a way of reading the original.</p>
