<summary><strong>ICCEPM 2025</strong> — Personality Traits as Predictors of Thermal Preference and Thermal Regulatory Intention &nbsp;<span style="font-weight:normal;color:#888;">(1st author)</span></summary>

<div style="margin-top:1rem;">
<img src="/assets/img/publications/iccepm2025_p01.jpg" alt="ICCEPM 2025" style="max-width:100%;margin-bottom:1rem;">
<p><strong>Authors:</strong> <strong>Gun KUK</strong> <em>(1st)</em>, Dahyun Jung, Hyuna Kang, Seunghoon Jung, Seungkeun Yeom, Jinwoo Choi, Juui Kim, Taehoon Hong</p>
<p><strong>Venue:</strong> 11th International Conference on Construction Engineering and Project Management — Jeju, Korea (Nov 17–20, 2025)</p>
<p><strong>Abstract.</strong> Occupants' thermal perception and behavior play a critical role in both thermal comfort and energy consumption. Machine learning models have been introduced to capture these individual differences and enable smart building systems. However, most existing models rely on physiological responses or self-reported thermal responses, which require intensive sensing and limit real-world scalability. To address this issue, this study investigates the potential of personality traits as stable, lightweight predictors of thermal preference (TP) and thermal regulatory intention (TRI). Data were collected from 30 healthy adults in an artificial climate chamber across four thermal conditions. Predictors included personality traits, physiological responses, and self-reported thermal responses, and TP and TRI were predicted using random forest classifiers. Results show that models based on personality traits consistently outperformed models based on physiological responses and self-reported thermal responses, especially in binary classification. Notably, openness to experience was included in all optimal feature combinations. These findings highlight the implication of personality traits for occupant-centric thermal modeling, offering a practical pathway for scalable and sensor-light prediction in smart building systems.</p>
<p><strong>Keywords:</strong> personality traits · thermal preference · thermal regulatory intention · machine learning · prediction</p>

<summary><strong>ISEC 2026</strong> — Comparative Analysis of Automated Ergonomic Risk Assessment Methods &nbsp;<span style="font-weight:normal;color:#888;">(1st author)</span></summary>

<div style="margin-top:1rem;">
<img src="/assets/img/publications/isec2026_p01.jpg" alt="ISEC 2026" style="max-width:100%;margin-bottom:1rem;">
<p><strong>Authors:</strong> <strong>Gun KUK</strong> <em>(1st)</em>, Dahyun Jung, Seungkeun Yeom, Jinwoo Choi, Taehoon Hong</p>
<p><strong>Venue:</strong> Proceedings of International Structural Engineering and Construction, 13(2), 2026</p>
<p><strong>Abstract.</strong> Recent studies have focused on automating ergonomic risk assessment methods in construction sites, where ergonomic risks are particularly high. However, comparative analyses of these automated indices remain relatively limited. This study investigates the inter-method consistency among automated implementations of Rapid Entire Body Assessment (REBA), Rapid Upper Limb Assessment (RULA), and Ovako Working Posture Analysis System (OWAS) — across rescaling schemes, task activities, and degrees of ergonomic risk factor incorporation. To this end, absolute agreement and ordinal trends were evaluated. The results indicate that: (i) incorporating more ergonomic risk factors reduces agreement in absolute action levels but does not change the relative ranking of action levels, and (ii) each index exhibits different sensitivity to individual risk factors. These findings suggest that inherent inter-method inconsistencies among automated indices cannot be resolved through automation refinement alone. Instead, the need for task-specific index selection is highlighted, and design requirements for new ergonomic risk assessment indices are outlined.</p>
<p><strong>Keywords:</strong> work-related musculoskeletal disorders · 3D pose estimation · posture assessment · agreement analysis · construction safety · computer vision</p>
</div>
</details>

<summary><em>Development of Cement-Free Ultra-High-Performance Concrete Using Powdered Calcium Hydroxide as Activator</em></summary>

<div style="margin-top:1rem;">
<img src="/assets/img/publications/uhpc_thesis_p01.jpg" alt="UHPC Thesis" style="max-width:100%;margin-bottom:1rem;">
<p><strong>Authors:</strong> <strong>Gun KUK</strong> <em>(co-1st)</em>, Doojin Kang, Taekguen Oh, Doo-Yeol Yoo</p>
<p><strong>Venue:</strong> Yonsei University, unpublished</p>
<p><strong>Abstract.</strong> This study investigates the influence of powdered calcium hydroxide (CH) content on the mechanical performance of ultra-high-performance concrete (UHPC) based on ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBFS). To this end, compressive and tensile strength experiments were conducted with six different CH weight ratios (2.5, 5, 7.5, 10, 12.5, and 15 wt%). Further analysis was conducted via crack pattern observation and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). The results indicate the following: (1) CH7.5 yielded the optimal performance; (2) CH7.5 achieved a compressive strength of 187.5 MPa, tensile strength of 18.6 MPa, strain capacity of 0.737%, and strain energy density of 113.2 kJ/m³, which are significantly higher than those of conventional cement-based UHPC; (3) all specimens exhibited both bridging and multiple-crack effects, with consistent trends observed between strain-hardening behavior and crack count; (4) CH7.5 showed the highest formation of hydration products contributing to strength development.</p>
<p><strong>Keywords:</strong> calcium hydroxide · mechanical performance · ultra-high-performance concrete · GGBFS · crack pattern · thermogravimetric analysis</p>
</div>
</details>
