Gun Kuk

Undergraduate Researcher · i-SUSCOME Lab, Architectural Engineering, Yonsei University

I am an undergraduate researcher in Architectural Engineering at Yonsei University, working with the i-SUSCOME Lab. My research sits at the intersection of building science, human–building interaction, physiological sensing, and machine learning.

I am especially interested in how buildings can understand people with less friction: not only through thermostat setpoints or one-time comfort surveys, but through signals, behavior, context, and individual traits that explain why different occupants experience the same environment differently. In my thermal-comfort work, I use chamber experiments, physiological signals such as EDA, ECG/HRV, SKT, and PPG, and machine-learning models to study thermal preference, thermal regulatory intention, and scalable occupant-centric prediction.

A second thread of my work asks how construction knowledge can be captured from the body. I am developing computer-vision and human-activity-recognition pipelines that turn construction workers’ posture, motion, and task behavior into data for ergonomic risk assessment, safety monitoring, robotic learning, and training support for junior workers.

Before focusing on research, I trained as an architectural designer. That background still shapes how I think: I care about models that are technically solid, but I also care about whether their results can be felt in actual rooms, workflows, and design decisions. I like research that connects experimental evidence, computational methods, and the everyday experience of people inside buildings.

Current Research
  • Precision-weighted thermal appraisal: modeling how personality and individual differences modulate thermal comfort in a large-scale chamber study.
  • Sensor-light thermal prediction: comparing personality traits, physiological responses, and subjective thermal responses as predictors of thermal preference and regulatory intention.
  • Construction ergonomics from vision: building an automated pipeline from 3D pose estimation to REBA, RULA, OWAS, and report generation.
  • Human activity recognition: converting skilled construction workers’ tacit movement patterns into data for robots, safety systems, and worker training.
